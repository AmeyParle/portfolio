Governance Portfolio

A governance program for Harbor Federal Bank, a fictional mid-size US bank, built against real regulation (SR 26-2, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, ECOA/Reg B) and real public data.

Framework · 26 Controls AI Agent Governance Control Framework
PDF ↗

SR 26-2, the 2026 bank model risk guidance, puts agentic AI outside its scope, so banks have to build this governance themselves. The framework makes every agent known, owned, bounded, supervised, auditable and stoppable.

  • 26 controls across six domains — inventory, identity and delegation, human oversight, testing, monitoring, incident response — each with its type, tiers and audit evidence, plus test procedures for key controls.
  • Risk tiering with autonomy levels and override rules, and proportionality by tier, from go-live approval rights to kill-switch testing.
  • Mapped to NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and SR 26-2, with a gap analysis of where those frameworks are silent on agents.
  • Applied to a bank collections agent and checked against two real incidents: the Replit production-database deletion and Moffatt v. Air Canada.

Merged · IETF Draft Repo · Sept 2026 RFC 8785 Conformance Vectors
GitHub ↗

An independent author-set for JSON canonicalization comparison semantics, merged into the draft-etcheverry-action-ref IETF draft repo. Zero-dependency verifier, 19 assertions.

  • Unicode normalization fails closed: identical-looking identifiers canonicalize to different bytes, so a relying party sees a conflict that isn't there.
  • Integers above 2^53 fail open: two different values serialize identically, so a relying party sees a match that isn't there, and nothing in the record shows it.
  • Each vector states its failure mode, so the risk is machine-readable rather than left to prose.
